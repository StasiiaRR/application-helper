# Cover Letter Guidelines

A general framework for a personal-narrative-first cover letter. No worked examples are
included here — supply your own experiences, contacts, and company specifics when drafting.

## Structure (4–5 paragraphs, ~450–550 words total)

1. **Opening hook (3–5 sentences).** Address a named recruiter/team. State the exact role
   and location applied for in sentence one. Frame the application as more than a job — a
   natural extension of a personal path. Name 2–3 personal qualities that define that path.
   Close the paragraph with a direct statement of readiness to bring that mindset to the company.

2. **Origin story (1 paragraph).** A personal narrative that explains *why this work matters
   to the candidate*, not just that they're qualified. Connect a formative experience to the
   sector/mission of the target company. Bridge into current professional experience and
   what it taught them (systems thinking, empathy, precision, leadership). End by naming a
   clear personal purpose that aligns with the role.

3. **Why this company (1 paragraph).** Cite specific, named company products, initiatives, or
   values. Where genuine, reference a real personal contact or conversation at the company as
   informal social proof (never invent one). Draw a line from the current role's
   responsibilities to what the target role needs. Optionally close on a light,
   brand-flavored line.

4. **The match / proof (1 paragraph).** Either continue the personal narrative with concrete
   examples, or pivot to "why we're a strong match" — restating fit between demonstrated
   skills and the company's needs. Use concrete, specific, quantified proof points rather
   than generic claims.

5. **Closing (2–3 sentences).** Thank them for considering the application. Express eagerness
   to discuss further. Sign off "Sincerely," + full name.

## Tone characteristics

- **Personal-narrative-first, credentials-second.** Lead with identity and story, not a
  bullet list of qualifications. Weave skills in as evidence of the story.
- **Emotionally resonant but controlled.** State vulnerable material plainly and briefly,
  then pivot to what it built in the candidate — never linger for sympathy alone.
- **First-person ownership language.** "I want to," "I am ready to," "I believe" —
  declarative, not tentative.
- **Specificity over generality.** Real names, real products, real numbers, real quotes from
  the company. Replace vague claims with a named instance.
- **Mission alignment framing.** Explicitly state the values overlap between candidate and
  company: company mission = candidate's personal mission.
- **Warm but professional register.** No jargon-heavy corporate speak; readable sentences
  with rhythm (short declaratives among longer ones for emphasis).
- **Confident closing, not eager-to-please.** Express openness to further conversation rather
  than asking permission or hedging.

## Universal template checklist

- [ ] Open with named recipient + exact role/title/location
- [ ] State personal qualities that define the candidate's path (2–3 traits)
- [ ] Include one formative personal story tied to the sector/mission
- [ ] Connect that story to a professional lesson or skill
- [ ] Name specific company products/initiatives/values (with a quote if possible)
- [ ] Name one real contact/conversation at the company (only if genuine)
- [ ] Include concrete, specific, named proof points (not generic adjectives)
- [ ] Explicitly state the mission/values overlap between candidate and company
- [ ] Close with gratitude + eagerness for further conversation
- [ ] Sign "Sincerely," + full name
- [ ] Keep to 4–5 paragraphs, ~450–550 words
